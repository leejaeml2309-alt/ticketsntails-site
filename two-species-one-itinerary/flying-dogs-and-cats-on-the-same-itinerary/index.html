Flying Dogs and Cats on the Same Itinerary

Published 21 November 2026

A dog and two cats can share a calendar and still not share a single set of official rules. On a journey from one country to another, the texts that apply to the dog and the texts that apply to the cats are not the same, and neither is written by this journal.

This note is general. It does not describe a flight we have taken, and it does not name an airline’s cabin limit as if it were universal. Every claim below was read on an official page on 21 November 2026.

At the border, the dog and the cats are not under one CDC form

CDC publishes detailed importation requirements for dogs. Those requirements depend on where the dog was vaccinated and which countries the dog has been in during the six months before entry into the United States. CDC, Bringing a Dog into the U.S. Verified 21 Nov 2026

For cats, the same agency’s animal-importation page requires that cats appear healthy upon arrival. Cats are subject to inspection and may be denied entry if they have evidence of a zoonotic disease. CDC does not require proof of rabies vaccination for cats to enter the United States, though it recommends vaccination and notes that the U.S. destination may have additional requirements. CDC, Bringing an Animal into the U.S. Verified 21 Nov 2026

USDA APHIS likewise treats the two species differently at import: it names CDC as the primary authority for dog imports, and it states that APHIS Veterinary Services does not have animal health requirements for bringing a pet cat into the United States from a foreign country. APHIS, Bring a Pet Dog into the United States Verified 21 Nov 2026 APHIS, Bring a Pet Cat into the United States Verified 21 Nov 2026

On one itinerary, that split is practical: the dog may need a CDC Dog Import Form and the conditions that go with it; the cats are inspected under a shorter CDC standard, and may still need papers that a state or an airline — not CDC — asks for. CDC, Bringing an Animal into the U.S. Verified 21 Nov 2026

In the air, the airline writes the pet policy

The U.S. Department of Transportation says some airlines allow pets in the cabin or in the cargo hold, that pet policies vary among airlines, and that you should check the airline’s policies before travel. A pet, DOT says, is separate and distinct from a service animal. U.S. DOT, Flying with a Pet Verified 21 Nov 2026

DOT also states that many airlines allow only cats and dogs as pets; that many place size and weight restrictions on pets in the cabin, and may require the pet to fit in a carrier under the seat; that some will not carry brachycephalic (short-nosed) breeds because of breathing risks; that fees may apply; that some restrict cargo travel in hot or cold weather; that seating may be limited (for example, bulkhead or emergency-row seats); and that some require a certified health record issued within 10 days of travel. Those are descriptions of what airlines may do, not a promise that any one airline will accept a particular dog and two cats in the cabin on the same flight. U.S. DOT, Flying with a Pet Verified 21 Nov 2026

The Federal Aviation Administration allows each airline to decide whether pets may travel in the passenger cabin. If the airline does allow it, the pet container is carry-on baggage under 14 CFR 121.589: it must be small enough to fit underneath the seat without blocking any person’s path to the main aisle, must be stowed properly before the last passenger entry door is closed, and must remain stowed while the airplane is moving on the airport surface and for take-off and landing. The FAA notes that airlines may also limit the number of pets in the cabin, the number that may accompany you, and may require the pet to remain in the container for the entire flight. FAA, Flying with Pets Verified 21 Nov 2026

Because those cabin limits and fees are set by the airline, a household travelling with a dog and two cats has to read the carrier’s own official pet pages — and, if needed, ask the airline — to learn whether all three animals can be accepted on the same flight, in the cabin, in cargo, or not at all. This journal cannot answer that in the airline’s place.

Do not confuse pets with service animals

FAA notes that even on a flight with no pets in the cabin, U.S. Department of Transportation rules in 14 CFR part 382 require U.S. airlines to allow passengers to fly with their service animals in the cabin. Service animals, that page says, are not pets; they are working animals that assist persons with disabilities. FAA, Flying with Pets Verified 21 Nov 2026

DOT’s own flying-with-a-pet page points to a separate service-animal page for that distinction. The animals in this journal — Future, Lili and Lilou — are being written about as pets, and the pet rules above are the ones that apply to them. U.S. DOT, Flying with a Pet Verified 21 Nov 2026
