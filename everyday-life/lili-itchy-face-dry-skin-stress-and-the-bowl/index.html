Lili’s Itchy Face: Dry Skin, Stress and What Goes in the Bowl

Published 24 November 2026

Lili has been scratching her face, and so far nobody has found an allergy to explain it.

Lili and Lilou are sisters, born in July 2016. They came from a pet shop in the Paris region, and at the end of 2016 they flew from France to Korea with Marc. Lili is the tortoiseshell; Lilou, smaller, is black and white. Ten years on, they share the same flat, the same routine and the same food. Lilou has none of this trouble.

Her ears have been checked and came back fine. The two leads we are following with her vet are dry skin and stress. This page is what we read while following them, source by source. It is a household story, not a diagnosis.

Talk to your vet. Nothing here is veterinary advice. A scratching cat needs to be examined, and any change of food, supplement or product should be agreed with the veterinarian who knows your cat.

A scratched face has many possible causes

The first thing we learned is how long the list is. Cornell’s Feline Health Center notes that the itchy eruptions of a food allergy primarily affect the head and neck. Cornell Feline Health Center, Food Allergies Checked 24 Nov 2026 VCA describes allergic itch in cats affecting the face, ears, belly, legs and paws, sometimes with recurrent skin or ear infections. VCA Animal Hospitals, Food Allergies in Cats Checked 24 Nov 2026

Ear mites make a cat scratch its ears almost without letup, and Cornell links the nonstop scratching to damage to the ears and face. Cornell Feline Health Center, Ear Mites Checked 24 Nov 2026 Cats allergic to flea saliva can scratch hard enough to hurt themselves quickly. Cornell Feline Health Center, Flea Allergy Checked 24 Nov 2026 Ringworm, a fungal infection, can show up as hair loss, scaling, dandruff and excessive grooming and scratching. Cornell Feline Health Center, Ringworm Checked 24 Nov 2026

Age matters too. Cornell describes hyperthyroidism as a common disease that mostly affects middle-aged and older cats, and lists an unkempt, matted or greasy coat among its possible signs. Cornell Feline Health Center, Hyperthyroidism in Cats Checked 24 Nov 2026 Lili is ten, which is one reason this is a question for her vet rather than for us.

Cornell also explains why the itch is worth treating early: scratching damages the skin, inflammation makes it itchier, and the cycle feeds itself. Working out the cause relies on the history, a physical examination and tests. Cornell Feline Health Center, Feline Skin Diseases Checked 24 Nov 2026 That is why a short log of when and where the cat scratches, with a few photos, is worth bringing to the vet. If nothing is found, Cornell’s dermatologist says some skin problems are best sorted out with a veterinary dermatologist. Cornell Feline Health Center, The Challenge of Skin Disorders Checked 24 Nov 2026

If food is suspected, the test is long and strict

VCA says the only reliable test for a food allergy in cats is an elimination diet trial lasting at least eight weeks, using a hydrolyzed-protein or novel-protein diet that your veterinarian prescribes — and nothing else: no treats, no supplements, not even a licked plate. VCA Animal Hospitals, Food Allergies in Cats Checked 24 Nov 2026 Cornell describes the same idea, with a novel diet fed alone for at least eight to ten weeks. Cornell Feline Health Center, Food Allergies Checked 24 Nov 2026

That matters for everything below. Any extra food — a bit of fish, a spoonful of something — can spoil a trial. Ask your vet before adding anything.

Dry skin, and a heated floor

In winter the flat runs on floor heating, and the air gets dry. A CatWatch article written with Cornell’s Feline Health Center says dry skin can come from home heating and low humidity in winter, and that adding a humidifier and regular grooming may be all a mild case needs. The same article is clear that itching, skin lesions or hair loss alongside the flakes call for a veterinary evaluation. CatWatch (with Cornell Feline Health Center), Dealing with Dry Skin Checked 24 Nov 2026

On grooming, VCA says regular brushing removes dead skin cells and spreads the skin’s natural oils along the hair, and that most healthy adult cats rarely need a bath. If a bath is needed, use a shampoo made for cats, never a human or baby shampoo. VCA Animal Hospitals, Grooming and Coat Care for Your Cat Checked 24 Nov 2026

We also keep water easy to reach. Cornell says some cats drink more from a fountain (preferences vary), that wet food and water added to food raise intake, and that in multi-cat homes one cat can be kept from the bowl by another — something to watch with two sisters. Cornell Feline Health Center, Hydration Checked 24 Nov 2026 Cornell does not present hydration as a cure for dry skin; it is simply part of looking after a cat.

Omega-3 and fish oil. Cornell’s dermatologist says a fatty acid supplement may help some itchy cats, is not 100 percent effective, and that too much can do harm, so the amount should come from your vet. Cornell Feline Health Center, The Challenge of Skin Disorders Checked 24 Nov 2026 VCA lists skin allergies and dermatitis among the uses of fish oil, warns that full effects can take several weeks, and notes possible interactions, for example with some anti-inflammatory drugs. VCA Animal Hospitals, Fish Oil Checked 24 Nov 2026 Cornell adds that supplements should never be given without a veterinarian’s approval. Cornell Feline Health Center, Feeding Your Cat Checked 24 Nov 2026 In short, fish oil is a conversation with the vet, not a kitchen experiment.

Stress, the other lead

The feline environmental needs guidelines from the AAFP and ISFM say cats often show little outward sign of stress, that much of it comes from needs not being met, and that predictability, familiarity and routine help a cat cope. They organise a healthy home around five “pillars”: a safe place, often raised; separate key resources (food, water, litter, scratching, play and rest areas); a chance to play and “hunt”; positive, consistent and predictable contact with people; and respect for the cat’s sense of smell. AAFP and ISFM Feline Environmental Needs Guidelines (PDF) Checked 24 Nov 2026

At home, that can mean simple things: a high hiding place of her own, her own food and water spots away from her sister’s, short play sessions at about the same time each day, and a stable routine.

Pheromone diffusers. The same guidelines say a synthetic feline facial pheromone analog can have a calming effect in unfamiliar or stressful situations. AAFP and ISFM Feline Environmental Needs Guidelines (PDF) Checked 24 Nov 2026 VCA explains that diffusers typically last about four weeks, that sprays go on blankets or carriers and never directly on the animal, and that the FDA does not review such non-drug products before sale — so ask your vet. VCA Animal Hospitals, Pheromones Checked 24 Nov 2026

Fish in the bowl, carefully

Our notes said “a little plain, cooked fish for her skin.” Here is what the sources actually support.

  • Fish can be the problem, not the cure. VCA lists fish, with beef, chicken and dairy, among the foods most often linked to food allergies in cats. VCA Animal Hospitals, Food Allergies in Cats Checked 24 Nov 2026
  • Treats stay small. Cornell’s rule of thumb is that treats should not exceed 10 to 15 percent of a cat’s daily calories. Cornell Feline Health Center, Feeding Your Cat Checked 24 Nov 2026
  • Never raw. Cornell does not recommend raw meat for cats because of infection risk. Cornell Feline Health Center, Feeding Your Cat Checked 24 Nov 2026 The Merck Veterinary Manual notes that raw fish contains thiaminases, enzymes that destroy thiamine (vitamin B1); cooking or canning destroys them. Merck Veterinary Manual, Nutritional Requirements and Related Diseases of Small Animals Checked 24 Nov 2026
  • Not human canned fish, and not much tuna. Cornell reports that some cats fed canned fish meant for people developed serious neurological disorders. Cornell Feline Health Center, Feeding Your Cat Checked 24 Nov 2026 Merck notes that cats given large quantities of canned tuna have developed vitamin E deficiency and pansteatitis, “yellow fat disease”. Merck Veterinary Manual, Nutritional Requirements and Related Diseases of Small Animals Checked 24 Nov 2026
  • No salt, no bones. ASPCA warns that excessive salt can make pets ill, and that bones can injure or block the digestive tract. ASPCA Animal Poison Control, People Foods to Avoid Feeding Your Pets Checked 24 Nov 2026

We found no veterinary source giving a weekly amount of fish that helps a cat’s skin, so we do not suggest one. While Lili’s vet is still looking at her skin, any fish is a question for the vet first.

What we left out: natto, goat’s milk and yogurt

Natto. It came up in our notes. We found no veterinary source that recommends natto for cats, and the sauce and mustard sold with it are salty — and ASPCA warns against excess salt. ASPCA Animal Poison Control, People Foods to Avoid Feeding Your Pets Checked 24 Nov 2026 It is not on this page as advice.

Goat’s milk and Greek yogurt. They come up often as gentle treats. The sources we read do not back them for cats. Cornell says milk is not generally recommended as a treat because many cats are lactose-intolerant and can develop digestive problems from dairy. Cornell Feline Health Center, Feeding Your Cat Checked 24 Nov 2026 ASPCA says pets lack significant amounts of lactase, so milk and other dairy-based products can cause diarrhea or other digestive upset, and it lists xylitol, a sweetener found in some foods, among the things to avoid. ASPCA Animal Poison Control, People Foods to Avoid Feeding Your Pets Checked 24 Nov 2026 VCA lists dairy among the foods linked to food allergies in cats. VCA Animal Hospitals, Food Allergies in Cats Checked 24 Nov 2026 We found no veterinary source recommending “pet” goat’s milk or yogurt for a cat’s skin, so we do not recommend them.

Where Lili is now

Lili is ten, and shares a flat and a feeding routine with a sister who has no such trouble. The two leads — dry skin and stress — are being worked through with her vet. If her vet’s findings change the picture, this page will say so.
