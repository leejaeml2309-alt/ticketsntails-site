Korea-to-U.S. Pet Move Checklist

Published 21 November 2026

A move from South Korea to the United States with a dog and cats is a sequence of official steps, not a single form. This list follows CDC, USDA APHIS and Korea’s Animal and Plant Quarantine Agency (APQA) as those pages read on 21 November 2026.

Print it, tick it, and re-open the official links before you travel. If a page has changed, follow the official page, not this list.

This page is not legal, veterinary or official advice. Rules change. Read the official page, and the airline’s own pages, before you travel.

1. Re-read the official lists first

  • Open CDC’s current high-risk countries for dog rabies list. On 21 November 2026, South Korea did not appear on that list. CDC states that if a country is not listed, it is not considered high risk for importing dog rabies into the United States, and that dogs which have not been in any listed country during the past six months can meet the requirements for dogs from dog rabies-free or low-risk countries. The list can change. Verified 21 Nov 2026
  • If the dog has been in any country that is on the high-risk list in the six months before U.S. entry, stop using the shorter low-risk list below and follow CDC’s pages for dogs from high-risk countries instead. This journal does not treat those extra steps as if they apply to every Korea-to-U.S. journey.
  • Confirm the destination state’s own rules. CDC says it does not require general certificates of health for pets to enter the United States, but that health certificates may be required for entry into some states or by airlines. CDC, Bringing an Animal into the U.S. Verified 21 Nov 2026

2. For the dog, if only in dog rabies-free or low-risk countries

CDC’s page for dogs from dog rabies-free or low-risk countries states that, starting 1 August 2024, the only required documentation for dogs that have been only in such countries in the past six months is the CDC Dog Import Form, plus the conditions below. CDC, Entry Requirements for Dogs from Dog-Rabies Free or Low-Risk Countries Verified 21 Nov 2026

  • Complete a free CDC Dog Import Form for each dog (one dog per form). After submission, confirm the email address; the receipt can be printed or shown on a phone. CDC, CDC Dog Import Form and Instructions Verified 21 Nov 2026
  • If the dog will travel by air, be ready to show the receipt to the airline before boarding, and to U.S. Customs and Border Protection on arrival. CDC Dog Import Form and Instructions Verified 21 Nov 2026
  • The dog must appear healthy upon arrival, be at least six months old at the time of entry, and have a microchip that can be detected with a universal scanner. CDC, low-risk entry requirements Verified 21 Nov 2026
  • Dogs that have been only in dog rabies-free or low-risk countries in the past six months may enter the United States at any airport, seaport, or land border crossing. CDC, low-risk entry requirements Verified 21 Nov 2026

3. For the cats

CDC’s dog-import paperwork does not apply to cats. The agency’s animal-importation page requires that cats appear healthy upon arrival. Cats are subject to inspection at ports of entry and may be denied entry if they have evidence of a zoonotic disease. CDC does not require proof of rabies vaccination for cats entering the United States, though it recommends that all cats be vaccinated against rabies, and the U.S. destination may have additional requirements. CDC, Bringing an Animal into the U.S. Verified 21 Nov 2026

  • Plan for each cat to be presentable for inspection and to appear healthy on arrival.
  • Check whether the airline or the destination state asks for a health certificate or other papers that CDC itself does not require for entry into the United States.
  • If a route includes Hawaii or Guam, read those local quarantine rules separately. CDC states that all cats arriving in Hawaii and in Guam, even from the U.S. mainland, are subject to locally imposed quarantine requirements. CDC, Bringing an Animal into the U.S. Verified 21 Nov 2026

4. USDA APHIS

APHIS states that CDC is the primary authority for dog imports into the United States, including dogs entering or returning. USDA APHIS, Pet Travel Verified 21 Nov 2026 APHIS, Bring a Pet Dog into the United States Verified 21 Nov 2026

APHIS Veterinary Services states that it does not have any animal health requirements related to bringing a pet cat into the United States from a foreign country, and that you should still check CDC, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service, and the destination state or territory. APHIS, Bring a Pet Cat into the United States Verified 21 Nov 2026

For dogs, APHIS Veterinary Services has additional requirements only if the dog is coming from a country affected by foot-and-mouth disease (FMD) or screwworm. This journal does not state whether South Korea is on those lists; open the APHIS dog-import page and follow it if it applies. APHIS, Bring a Pet Dog into the United States Verified 21 Nov 2026

  • Re-read the APHIS dog and cat import pages, and any FMD or screwworm conditions, close to the travel date.

5. Korea’s APQA export steps

Korea’s Animal and Plant Quarantine Agency (APQA) English page on dogs and cats export quarantine, opened on 21 November 2026, says you must meet the quarantine requirements of the destination country, and that you should check those conditions with the embassy or the animal quarantine authority. It says to consult a veterinarian at an animal hospital if you need a rabies vaccination certificate and a health certificate. APQA, Dogs and Cats Export Quarantine Verified 21 Nov 2026

The same page says you apply for the quarantine certificate by visiting an APQA office at the airport or the port, with the animals and the necessary documents, such as the vaccination certificate and the health certificate. An inspector issues the veterinary certificate after assessing the documents and carrying out clinical tests. The fee stated on that page is 10,000 won per case. Incheon International Airport Terminal 1 and Terminal 2 offices are listed as open Monday to Sunday, including public holidays, from 09:00 to 18:00, with arrival before 17:30 required; an appointment is recommended. APQA, Dogs and Cats Export Quarantine Verified 21 Nov 2026

APQA also publishes a country-by-country pet-export guide, which includes an entry for the United States (non-commercial). That page warns that each country’s rules can change without notice. APQA, country-specific pet export guidance Verified 21 Nov 2026

  • Obtain, from a veterinarian, the rabies vaccination and health certificates APQA asks you to bring, if they are needed for your animals.
  • Book or confirm an APQA appointment where the office requires or recommends one, and arrive with the animals before the cut-off time listed for that office.
  • Apply in person at the relevant APQA airport or port office, pay the stated fee, and keep the veterinary certificate with the animals.

The older qia.go.kr hostname did not load reliably when we checked it on 21 November 2026 (connection or HTTP 500 errors). The pages above were opened successfully on www.apqa.go.kr.

6. The airline’s own pages

The U.S. Department of Transportation says pet policies vary among airlines, and that you should check the airline’s policies before travel. Some airlines require a certified health record issued within 10 days of travel. U.S. DOT, Flying with a Pet Verified 21 Nov 2026

If the airline allows a pet in the cabin, the FAA treats the pet container as carry-on baggage: it must fit under the seat without blocking the aisle, be stowed before the last passenger door is closed, and remain stowed while the aircraft is moving on the ground and for take-off and landing. FAA, Flying with Pets Verified 21 Nov 2026

  • Read the official pet pages of the airline you will actually fly, including cabin or cargo acceptance, fees, and any health certificate the airline requires.
